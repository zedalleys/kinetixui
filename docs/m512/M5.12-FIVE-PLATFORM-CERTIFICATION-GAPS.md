## Gap 1 — `Acceptance/` is run by no workflow

`grep -rln 'Acceptance' .github/workflows/` returns **nothing**. `Native (SwiftUI)` runs `swift build`
and `swift test` against the SPM package only, and `Package.swift` declares one
`.testTarget(name: "KinetixUITests")`. Both acceptance hosts live outside it:

| host | what it proves | who runs it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Acceptance/Selection/` (#636) | forward/reverse travel, measured from a recording by `measure-selection.py` (ffmpeg raw frames, 402×874 @ 60fps) | a person, by hand, per its README |
| `Acceptance/Sheet/` (#637) | four XCUITests requiring a **new utterance with the exact target label** after the Sheet leaves the tree, including a reduced-motion case and an RTL rapid-reopen case asserting *zero* restoration requests while reopened and exactly one at final closure | a person, on an iOS 27 simulator with VoiceOver, per its README |

This is one structural gap with two instances, and it is the reason both PRs' strongest evidence is
invisible to CI.

## Gap 2 — #637 adds no CI-executed test at all

`git diff --name-only origin/main..stack` over the #633 + #637 stack touches exactly one file under
`Sources/` or `Tests/`: **`packages/ui-swiftui/Sources/KinetixUI/Sheet.swift`.** Everything else new is
under `Acceptance/Sheet/`, which SPM ignores. So the Sheet accessibility change is **compile-checked by
`swift build` and nothing more** in CI.

Its acceptance design is good — the README even specifies a negative control (`suppressRestore: true`,
after which the exact-target speech assertion must fail while the scope and dismissal still execute).
That design was **read, not run.** No simulator result is claimed, and simulator VoiceOver would not be
physical acceptance even if it had been.

## Gap 3 — SwiftUI's selection indicator has no direction-named test

Measured, with the gate's exact words:

```
✗ SwiftUI: no test in packages/ui-swiftui/Tests/KinetixUITests/SelectionIndicatorTests.swift
  is NAMED for the forward direction.
    Found: "test Forward And Backward Travel Require Stable Geometry",
           "test Reduced Motion Removes Travel Even When Layout Is Stable"
```

Neither name can be fixed by renaming, because
`KinetixSelectionIndicator.animation(reduceMotion:stableGeometry:)` is **direction-agnostic**. Closing
this needs two genuinely direction-named XCTests, which needs a seam that distinguishes the directions
— work that belongs on #636, whose branch was not modified.

Until then SwiftUI is a **declared gap** (`KNOWN_GAPS` rises 9 → 10) and is **not** a registered
`selection-indicator` consumer. `check:native-motion` reports `37 component(s) across 3 platform(s)
wired to the reader's setting and tested in both directions; 10 animated component(s) remain declared
gaps`. **Three platforms, not five.**

### The declared gap's measured cost

| control | observed |
| --- | --- |
| remove the `KNOWN_GAPS` entry | exit 1 — the entry is load-bearing, not decorative |
| with the gap declared, delete the reduced-motion guard | **exit 0** — this gate no longer catches it |

Partly mitigated from the same files: `SelectionIndicatorTests.swift` asserts
`animation(reduceMotion: true, stableGeometry: true)` is nil, so that mutation reddens the SwiftUI
suite, and `Native (SwiftUI)` is green on #636. The protection lives in the platform's suite, not in
the bookkeeping gate. That is a real but bounded loss, and it was measured rather than assumed.

## Gap 4 — no physical-device acceptance, on any platform

Not attempted and not claimable here. The outstanding items, carried forward unchanged: physical iOS
VoiceOver acceptance, physical Android TalkBack acceptance, macOS VoiceOver, and a full integrated
build across all five platforms.

## What a five-platform parity claim would need

1. Two direction-named XCTests for SwiftUI's selection indicator, and its registration as a contract
   consumer (removing the declared gap) — then `check:native-motion` reads four platforms.
2. Flutter and Compose suites executed on hosted CI at the integrated head, not at their PR heads.
3. The two `Acceptance/` hosts either wired into a macOS workflow, or their results recorded as dated,
   named, hand-run evidence that a gate can point at.
4. Physical-device acceptance for iOS and Android.

Until all four, the honest statement is the one `check:native-motion` already prints: **three
platforms wired and tested in both directions, ten declared gaps, which is a roadmap and not a
failure.**
